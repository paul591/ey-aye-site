Do you need a chatbot? Nay. Here's what to do instead.

If you run a small business in Yorkshire, someone has tried to sell you a chatbot this year. Possibly this week. Here's what it actually does, why your customers won't use it, and the three things that will save you more time for less money.

[Hero: a shop or clinic front desk with a real person on the phone, laptop open, a half-drunk mug of tea. No screens with robots.]

What a chatbot actually is

Strip the sales pitch off and a website chatbot is a text box that tries to guess what a visitor wants, from a list of answers somebody typed in once. The newer ones use ChatGPT or similar underneath, which makes them chattier, not more useful. It still doesn't know your diary, your prices this month, or that Thursday is the day the van's in for its MOT.

The £200-a-month "AI employee" being pushed in Facebook ads is usually exactly this: a chat window, a logo, and a twelve-month contract. The clever part is the contract.

Why your customers won't use it

Think about the last time you used one. You typed a question, it gave you a paragraph that didn't quite answer it, and you went looking for a phone number. That's everyone. For a local business the whole point of being local is that a person answers. A chatbot takes your one real advantage over the big firms and swaps it for the thing people dislike about the big firms.

There's a second cost. Every question the chatbot fumbles is a customer who now thinks your business is a bit careless. You never find out, because they just leave.

What saves time instead

The time you're losing isn't on the website. It's in the admin behind it. These three things are where AI pays for itself in a business of two to twenty people, and none of them need a new subscription.

1. The morning brief

Before the first customer walks in, a one-page note: who's booked today, who's new, who's back and what they said last time, who still owes you for last month. It reads your booking system and your invoices and writes the brief itself. Five minutes over a brew replaces twenty minutes of clicking round three logins. We built this first for Bodyworks by Corey, a clinic in Harrogate, because it was the thing that ate the start of every day.

2. The quote that writes itself

Trades and services lose more work to slow quotes than to bad ones. A quote written in the van, from a photo and a few words, sent before you've driven off, wins the job more often than a better quote sent on Sunday night. The AI drafts it from your own past quotes so it sounds like you and prices like you. You still read it before it goes. That's the rule.

3. The Friday chase

Unpaid invoices get chased on a Friday, politely, in your words, with the amount and the original invoice attached. It's the most boring job in the business and the one most often skipped. Letting the AI write the reminders, and you press send, gets the money in without the awkwardness.

What this costs

Less than the chatbot. The tools you already pay for, Claude or ChatGPT at a tenner a month, can do a surprising amount of this once someone shows you how. That's what our half-day is for: four hours with your own data, and you leave having built the first one yourself. When a job needs the AI plugged into your bookings or your accounts properly, that's a fixed-price build, and you own it afterwards.

And if none of it would pay off in your business, you'll be told that too. It happens. Some businesses are already as efficient as they're going to get, and the honest answer is to leave them alone.
