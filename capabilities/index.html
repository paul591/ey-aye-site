What we can build. All of it, in plain English.

The home page keeps things simple on purpose. This page is the full list, for anyone who wants to know exactly what's possible before they book an hour. Every one of these has been built, either for a client or for one of our own businesses. Each comes with an aye and a nay, because knowing where a thing stops is half of knowing what it's for.

01

Things that read for you

The inbox, the supplier PDFs, the forms, the scans of handwritten job sheets. An AI reads them, pulls out what matters, and either files it where it belongs or puts a one-line summary in front of you. Email gets sorted into "needs you", "needs someone else" and "needs nothing". Supplier delivery dates get lifted out of attachments and into your job system. Invoices get matched to the orders they belong to.

This is usually the first thing that pays off in a business, because it's the first thing that's eating the morning.

ayeit can read a PDF, a photo of a form, a CSV from your bank, an email thread.
nayit won't reply to anyone or move money without you seeing it first.
EmailPDF & WordCSVScansSupplier portals
Example: the morning brief reads the night's bookings and intake forms before the first client.
02

Things that write for you

Quotes written in the van from a photo and three words. Proposals that sound like you, because they're built from the last twenty you sent. Product descriptions, customer replies, the monthly report nobody enjoys writing. The AI drafts from your own past work, in your voice, and you read it before it goes.

The trick isn't the writing, it's the reading first: it only sounds like you if it has seen how you do it.

ayeit can draft the quote, the reply, the report, the listing, in your words.
nayit doesn't press send. That's the rule, and it's a good one.
QuotesProposalsCustomer repliesProduct copyReports
Example: Purple Helmet runs its product copy, campaigns and customer replies this way.
03

One place to ask

Your bookings are in one system, your invoices in another, the customer's last email in a third, the stock in a spreadsheet. We join them up so an AI can see all of them at once, read-only where it should be, and you ask it the way you'd ask a good office manager: "Who's in this week that still owes us?" "What did we quote the Smiths last time?" "Which lines haven't sold since spring?"

Technically this is a mix of connecting to your systems directly and teaching the AI to look things up in your documents rather than guess. You don't need to know that. You need to know every answer shows where it came from.

ayeit can see bookings, accounts, email, CRM, stock, job sheets and your documents together.
nayit can't see what isn't written down anywhere. Paper stays paper until it's scanned.
WixShopifyXeroQuickBooksHubSpotGoogle DriveSharePointSQLJob systems
Example: Pit Wall joins Shopify, Meta Ads, Klaviyo and print costs. Loftsoft Racing does it for race data.
04

Agents that do the job

Once the AI can read your systems, it can act in them. An order comes in: check payment, check stock, book the courier, print the label, email the tracking, post it to the accounts. An invoice arrives: match it to the purchase order, flag the difference, queue the payment for approval. A lead goes quiet: draft the follow-up, wait a week, nudge again, tell you when they bite.

Every one of these has a step where a person approves. You decide which step. For a tracking email, probably none. For a refund, always.

ayeorder-to-dispatch, invoice match and chase, lead follow-up, the Friday reminders, supplier ETA tracking.
nayit won't touch money, refunds or a customer complaint without a person's say-so.
ShopifyXeroCourier APIsCRMEmailCalendar
Example: the Friday chase and the quote-from-the-van on the chatbot post.
05

Dashboards and apps your team actually opens

Not forty charts. One page with the numbers that matter this week, where each one came from, and what to do about it. Net sales against last month. Margin by product after the real costs. The cash snapshot at half eight. A shared action plan the team can tick off. Built as a web page or a small app, with logins if different people should see different things.

The AI part is the layer that reads the page every Monday and writes down what it would do next. The dashboard shows you the numbers; the notes tell you what they mean.

ayelive numbers, margin after real costs, a weekly written verdict, a plan that saves for everyone.
nayno vanity metrics. If a number doesn't change a decision, it doesn't go on the page.
Web dashboardsRole-based accessMobile-friendlyWeekly AI notes

07

Machines, sensors and the data they throw off

This is the one nobody else local does. Thirty years of engine control units, dynamometers and race-car telemetry means we're comfortable with a log file from a CNC machine, a compressor's run hours, a van's tracker feed or a lab instrument's export. The AI reads the stream, spots what's drifting, and tells you before the thing fails rather than after.

If you make, fix or run physical things in Yorkshire and you've got data coming off them that nobody looks at, this is probably where the money is.

08

Keeping it safe

Every build gets the same five rules whether it's a clinic or a factory. The AI gets the least access it needs, read-only wherever it can be. The steps that matter have a person approving them. Every action is logged with what it read and why it did what it did. Your data stays in your systems, in the UK where the provider allows it, and nothing is used to train anyone's model. If a build can't meet all five, it doesn't get built.

ayeleast privilege, approval gates, an audit trail, UK data residency, no training on your data, a rule sheet your staff can follow.
nayno customer data in a consumer AI app, ever. That one's non-negotiable.
UK GDPRAccess controlAudit logApprovalsStaff training

09

Opening your own software to AI

If you already know what you need, this is the grown-up version. For firms with their own software, a big API, or systems that nothing off the shelf will talk to, we build the bespoke connection that lets an AI model use them safely: what's called an MCP server. It's the same work the founder has shipped for an automotive calibration platform used by major car makers, and the same thing running under Loftsoft Racing.

It comes with the governance to match: who the AI can act as, what it can see, what it must ask about, and a record of everything it did. Happy to talk at that level from the first conversation.

ayebespoke MCP servers over your API or database, permission models, audit, deployment inside your own estate.
naywe won't build one until the governance question has an answer. It's the first thing we ask.
MCPREST & GraphQLSQLOAuthOn-prem or cloud
Example: the automotive platform (under NDA) and Loftsoft Racing.
